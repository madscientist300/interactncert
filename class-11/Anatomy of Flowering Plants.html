Topic 1

The three tissue systems

The study of internal structure of plants is anatomy. Different organs differ inside, monocots and dicots differ from each other, and internal structure shows adaptations to the environment.

Tissues can be grouped by where they lie in the plant. On the basis of structure and location there are three tissue systems: epidermal, ground (fundamental) and vascular (conducting).

Topic 2

Epidermal tissue system and stomata

The epidermis is the outermost layer of the primary plant body: elongated, compactly arranged cells in a continuous layer, usually single-layered. The cells are parenchymatous, with a little cytoplasm lining the wall and a large vacuole. Outside is often a waxy, thick cuticle that prevents water loss; the cuticle is absent in roots.

Stomata in the epidermis of leaves regulate transpiration and gaseous exchange. Each stoma has two guard cells around a stomatal pore.

NEET trapGuard cell walls: the outer walls (away from the pore) are thin; the inner walls (towards the pore) are highly thickened. Guard cells have chloroplasts. The pore, guard cells and surrounding subsidiary cells together form the stomatal apparatus.
RememberRoot hairs are unicellular outgrowths of epidermal cells that absorb water and minerals. On the stem, epidermal hairs are trichomes: usually multicellular, branched or unbranched, soft or stiff, sometimes secretory. They help prevent water loss by transpiration.
Topic 3

Ground tissue and vascular bundles

The ground tissue is everything except the epidermis and vascular bundles. It is made of the simple tissues parenchyma, collenchyma and sclerenchyma. Parenchyma lies in the cortex, pericycle, pith and medullary rays of primary stems and roots. In leaves, the ground tissue is thin-walled, chloroplast-containing mesophyll.

The vascular system is made of the complex tissues phloem and xylem, which together form vascular bundles. Bundles are classified by the presence of cambium and the position of xylem and phloem.

Topic 4

Dicot and monocot roots

Tissue organisation is best studied in transverse sections of the mature zones. NCERT's dicot root is the sunflower. Moving inward: epiblema with unicellular root hairs; cortex of thin-walled parenchyma with intercellular spaces; endodermis; pericycle; vascular bundles; pith.

Topic 5

Dicot and monocot stems

In a young dicot stem, the cortex (between epidermis and pericycle) has three sub-zones: hypodermis of collenchyma, cortical parenchyma, and endodermis. In a monocot stem the bundles are scattered through a large ground tissue.
